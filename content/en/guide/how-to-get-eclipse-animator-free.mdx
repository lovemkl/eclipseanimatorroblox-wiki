## How the Free Claim Worked

During the launch window — approximately three days from the plugin's release in mid-October 2026 — the Eclipse Animator Roblox plugin was free to claim from the Roblox Creator Store. ## What Claiming Actually Does to Your Account

The permanence is the part worth understanding precisely, because it is unusual. Claiming during the window tied the Eclipse Animator Roblox plugin to your Roblox account permanently — not to a device, not to a Studio installation, not to a subscription. The practical consequences:

- **It follows your account anywhere.** Sign into Studio on any machine — home desktop, laptop, school computer — and your claimed plugins are available. The claim lives in your inventory, not your hardware.
- **Updates flow to claimed copies.** When the developers ship plugin updates, claimed owners receive them like any plugin holder. The free price did not freeze you on a launch build.
- **The claim survives the price change.** When the window closed and the listing went paid-only, claimed owners noticed nothing — no prompts, no expiry, no "your trial has ended." The plugin simply remains theirs.

The one maintenance task claiming creates is account hygiene: the permanence of your Eclipse Animator Roblox copy is only as durable as the security of the Roblox account holding it. Strong password, verified email, two-factor enabled — the standard kit. This is not a plugin-specific risk; it is the general truth that digital permanence is account permanence.

## What the Paid-Only Switch Changed

When the claim window closed, the Creator Store listing moved the plugin to paid-only status. The switch changed exactly one thing: the price of admission for people who had not claimed. The feature set, the update pipeline and the ownership experience stayed identical across free claimers and paying buyers — there are not two tiers of Eclipse Animator Roblox, only two prices that were ever charged for the same one.

For the community, the switch created a natural sorting. Permanent free owners animate with the season's most talked-about tool at a cost of one well-timed click. Everyone else faces the actual decision this wiki exists to inform: is the paid price worth it for your volume of work? ## If You Missed the Window: Your Options

The honest menu for latecomers, in the order most people should consider it:

**Buy the plugin.** If animation is a daily or weekly part of your Roblox work, the calculus is straightforward — the capability set (IK toggle, unparenting, live editing, any-animation-type support) is aimed squarely at working animators, and working animators amortize tool costs fast. Check the live listing for the current price; this wiki records the situation but does not freeze prices in text.

**Use the free Moon Animator 2 build.** The open-source release of Moon Animator 2 lives on GitHub and imports into Studio as a local plugin at no cost. It lacks Eclipse Animator's IK-first workflow and live editing, but it is a capable multi-rig animator with years of tutorials behind it — the comparison page details the trade.

**Master the built-in Animation Editor.** Studio's native editor is free forever and covers single-rig keyframe animation competently. For animators whose needs are simple, "free and adequate" beats "excellent and paid" — the built-in comparison page maps exactly where adequate ends.

**Watch for future windows.** Launch-window pricing is a pattern, not a law; whether the Eclipse Animator Roblox developers ever reprise a free period is their call, and this page's tracker updates if they do. Hoping is not a strategy, but watching costs nothing.
